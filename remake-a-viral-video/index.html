How to remake a viral video for your brand — without copying it

Copying a viral video gets you a worse version of someone else's post. Remaking its format gets you a new video that works for the same reasons the original did.

What actually made it work

Strip any viral video down and what carried it is rarely the topic. It is the format: the hook's shape, how long each beat runs, where the turn lands, what the camera does while the mouth talks. The topic is the paint. The format is the chassis.

This is why a study-hacks video and an EMT-prep video can be the same video: “How I got a 1550 on the SAT” and “I went from failing my medical assessment to a 96% in two weeks” share a chassis — cold claim, fast beats, late turn — with nothing else in common.

Keep, replace, drop

Keep: the hook's shape, the beat count and their lengths, the turn's position, the energy of the delivery, the shot rhythm.

Replace: every word, the topic, the product, the setting, anything that names the original's niche.

Drop: the audio if it carried the original (trending sounds age fast), and any beat whose only job was the original's inside joke.

The line between remake and copy

If someone who saw the original would call yours a response, you remade it. If they would call it a repost, you copied it. The test is whether your version survives on its own for a viewer who never saw the source — a remade format does, because the format was never the part anyone recognizes.
