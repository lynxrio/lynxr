Finding UGC brand deals

How to find brands that hire UGC creators, how to pitch them, and how to get booked again. 14 answers so far.

How do you write a UGC pitch email to a brand?

A UGC pitch email gives a brand one specific video idea for its product, links to your portfolio as proof, and ends with a single clear ask. Keep it short enough to read on a phone. Name the product, describe the opening seconds of the video you would make, and cut your life story, your follower numbers and any list of services.

How do you find brands that hire UGC creators?

Find UGC brands by looking for ones that already post creator-style videos, then checking which of them sell products you use or could film well. Keep a short list of those brands with a contact and a reason for each, and pitch a few at a time so every message is specific.

What should you send a brand with a UGC pitch?

Send a brand one portfolio link, one sample that matches its kind of product, and one written hook idea for its product, then offer rates when they ask. Everything else can wait. A small, matched package is quick to judge, and it shows you have looked at what the brand sells.

How do you follow up when a brand doesn't reply to a UGC pitch?

Follow up on a UGC pitch at most twice, in the same thread, and add one new thing each time, such as a fresh hook idea or a matching sample. Leave a gap you have decided in advance between messages, then send a short closing note and move the brand to a later list.

How do you get a brand to book you again for UGC?

To get a brand to book you again, make the first job easy to manage and then propose the next video yourself. Deliver on time with clearly named files, include a spare hook, ask what worked, and follow up with one concrete idea built on that answer, so saying yes takes the brand very little effort.

Can you pitch a brand whose product you haven't tried?

You can pitch a brand whose product you haven't tried, as long as the pitch says so plainly and offers to use it before you script anything. Pitch the idea and the format, ask for a sample, and keep every line in the finished video to what you have seen for yourself.

What makes a brand choose one UGC creator over another?

A brand chooses between UGC creators based on what it can see and check: your opening seconds, your audio, whether your samples follow a brief, and how clearly you reply. You cannot control who else applies, but you can make each of those signals easy to judge in the short time someone spends on your work.

How do you reply when a brand offers only free product?

Reply to a free-product offer by deciding first whether it is worth your time, then answering politely and clearly either way. Thank the brand, say what you can offer for product alone or what you need to take the job on, and keep the door open so a paid job can follow later.

How do you spot a UGC brand deal that isn't worth taking?

A UGC brand deal is not worth taking when you cannot say exactly what you are making, how many changes are included and when it is due. Watch for a scope that keeps growing, revisions with no limit, pressure to fake use of the product, and answers that go vague when you ask a direct question.

How do you turn a one-off UGC job into regular work?

To turn a one-off UGC job into regular work, send a few follow-up ideas after delivery, then propose one simple repeat arrangement with a set rhythm and scope. A standing plan gives the brand one decision instead of a fresh pitch to weigh every time, and keeping the first version small makes it easy to try.

How do you say no to a UGC job politely?

To turn down a UGC job politely, reply soon, thank the brand by name, give one short honest reason, and say what would make a yes possible next time. Keep it to a few lines, skip the long apology, and leave a specific door open so the brand knows a later job is welcome.

How do you ask a brand for feedback after a job?

Ask for feedback in one short message sent soon after final delivery, with two or three specific questions the brand can answer in a line each. Make it easy to reply, thank them whatever they say, and turn the answers into changes you can show in your next pitch.

How do you introduce yourself to a brand you already buy from?

Open with one specific, true detail about how you use the product, then offer a video idea built on that habit and link your samples. Your real routine is the part no other pitch can copy, so lead with it instead of a general introduction, and keep every claim to what you have actually done.

How do you reply when a brand asks for your rates first?

Reply to a brand that asks for your rates first by thanking them, asking a few short questions about the job, and promising a quote once you have the answers. Scope decides the work, so a quote built on it is one you can stand behind, and the questions show the brand you take the job seriously.
