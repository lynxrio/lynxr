How do you know if your UGC hook is too slow?

Your UGC hook is too slow if the first thing a viewer sees and hears is not yet the problem, the picture or the question the video is about. Watch the opening with the sound off and then with your eyes closed. If either pass starts with a greeting, a setup line or you getting into position, cut until it does not.

What are the signs that a UGC hook is too slow?

A hook is too slow when anything comes before the line or picture that makes the video worth watching.

The hook has one job: give a stranger a reason to stay for the next line. Anything you say or do before that reason arrives is a wait the viewer did not ask for. These are the patterns worth looking for in your own footage:

  • You open with a greeting, your name or a line like so I wanted to talk about something.
  • The first frame shows you reaching for the phone, settling into the chair or taking a breath.
  • The product or the problem only appears after you have explained why you are filming.
  • The interesting word sits at the end of a long sentence instead of near the front.
  • The on-screen text says the same thing as your voice, so nothing new reaches the viewer.

If your opener matches any of these, the fix is rarely a new idea. It is the same idea, moved forward. Once the dead air is gone, the guide to writing a scroll-stopping hook helps you choose which kind of opener to sharpen.

How do you test your own hook before sending it?

Watch the opening three ways: sound off, eyes closed, and on a phone held at arm's length, as a stranger would see it.

Each pass checks a different channel, because a viewer might meet your video through only one of them. With the sound off, ask whether the first frame shows something specific. With your eyes closed, ask whether the first words name a situation. At arm's length, ask whether you can tell what the video is about before you would naturally flick away. Write down the exact moment each pass gets interesting, then compare it with where the clip starts. The gap between the two is what to cut.

A second check is to read your script aloud and stop after the first sentence. If someone hearing only that sentence could not guess the topic, the sentence is a warm-up. The parts of a UGC video script shows what the setup is for, so you can move the warm-up there or delete it.

What should you cut from a slow opening?

Cut everything that explains, introduces or prepares, and keep only the words and picture that show the situation.

Slow openingWhat to do with it
Okay so, hi, I have been meaning to show you thisDelete it. Start on the next sentence.
A long sentence with the key word at the endFlip it so the specific detail comes first.
You walking into frame before you speakStart the clip once you are already in position and talking.
Background on why you bought the productMove it to the setup, after the viewer knows the problem.
On-screen text that repeats your first lineReplace it with a detail the voice does not give.

If a cut leaves the hook leaning on the line after it, that is fine. The payoff can arrive later as long as the opener raises the question it answers.

Where should the clip actually start?

Start the clip mid-action or mid-sentence, at the first frame where the problem or the product is already visible.

When you film, roll early and keep talking past the line, so the edit has room to trim both ends. Then in the edit, scrub to the frame where your mouth starts the hook line or your hand is already on the product, and cut there. Starting a fraction late is better than a fraction early, because a clipped breath reads as energy and a pause reads as hesitation. If the brief asks for a specific first shot, start on that shot and apply the same rule to it. Product in the first frame goes further on opening with the object itself, and visual hooks helps when the picture is doing the work.

What does a fixed hook look like in a full script?

A fixed hook names the situation in the first line and shows it in the first frame, and the setup handles everything else.

The slow draft of this video opened with: Hi, so I have been trying to read more before bed and I found something that helps. Everything useful in that sentence is in the last few words. The version below moves the situation to the front and lets the camera show it.
