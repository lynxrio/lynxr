How do you plan your first week as a UGC creator?

Plan your first week as a UGC creator around one finished sample video, not around finding clients. Spend the early days studying formats and setting up a filming spot, the middle days scripting and filming, and the last days editing, reviewing and putting the result somewhere a brand could see it.

Published 15 November 2026

What should your first week of UGC actually aim for?

Aim to finish one sample video you would be happy to show a brand, plus the notes and setup that make the second one easier.

A single finished video teaches you the whole job: picking an idea, writing it, filming it, cutting it and judging it. Five half-finished ones teach you only the parts you enjoy. So the week is built backwards from one deliverable, and every day either feeds it or makes the next one faster.

It helps to know what the work is before you plan it. If you are still unsure, read what a UGC creator is first, because the week below assumes you are making videos for brands to use, not growing an account of your own.

What does a day-by-day plan for the first week look like?

Give each day one job and one thing you can point to at the end of it, so you always know whether the day is done.

DayThe one jobWhat you have at the end
Day oneWatch short videos in your area and write down the shape of each, not the topic.A short list of formats you could film at home.
Day twoSet up a filming spot and test it with your phone, using what you already own.A spot with light facing you, a quiet room and a test clip you checked.
Day threePick a product you own and write yourself a short brief for it.A brief with one message, the shots you want and what you will deliver.
Day fourWrite the script and three different hooks for it.A script laid out with what you say beside what the camera sees.
Day fiveFilm every line and every hook, plus a few close shots of the product.All the raw footage, checked for sound and focus before you put the phone down.
Days six and sevenEdit one version, fix its single weakest part, then put it somewhere easy to share.A finished sample, a link you could send to a brand, and a note of what to change next time.

On day one, the guide to what a UGC video format is shows you how to separate a video's format from its subject, which is the habit the rest of the week relies on. On day four, borrow the structure from a UGC script template that holds up instead of starting from a blank page.

How do you fit the plan around the rest of your life?

Pick one fixed slot each day that you can protect, and size each day's job to fit that slot rather than stretching the slot to fit the job.

A short slot you keep every day beats a long one you skip, because the plan depends on each day handing something to the next. If a day runs over, carry the leftover into tomorrow's slot and push the rest back one day. Do not double up, because rushing the filming day to catch up is the quickest way to end up reshooting.

Filming is the one day that needs the most care. Choose the day when your light is best and the house is quietest, and move the other days around it.

What should you leave out of your first week?

Leave out anything that does not help you finish the sample, even when it feels like progress.

  • Shopping for gear. Film with what you have, and let the test clip from day two tell you whether anything is worth replacing.
  • Pitching brands. A pitch with nothing to show leaves you nothing to follow up with, so wait until you have a link.
  • Designing a logo or a full website. A single page or folder with your sample and a way to contact you is enough to start.
  • Filming several products at once. One product keeps the week focused, and the second sample can cover a different one.
  • Comparing your first video with someone's best. Compare it with your own test clip from day two instead, because that is the gap you can actually close.

How do you check whether the week worked?

The week worked if you can answer yes to a short set of questions about the sample and about how the next video will go.

  • Is there one finished video, edited and exported, that you would send to a brand today?
  • Does its hook make sense to someone who has never seen the product?
  • Do you have the other two hooks filmed, so you could swap one in without reshooting?
  • Can you say in one sentence what you would do differently in the next video?
  • Is the next product chosen and the next brief written, even roughly?

A no on any of these is not a failure. It is the job for the first day of next week, which is more useful than starting the second week with a fresh plan.

What should come after the first week?

Repeat the same week with a new product and a different format, so each round adds range instead of more of the same video.

The second round goes faster, because the filming spot is set up and the brief and script are templates now. Use the time you save to change one thing on purpose, such as a new format or a harder product to show.
