How do you ask a brand for an extension on a UGC deadline?

Ask for a deadline extension as soon as you know you'll miss the date, in one short message with the reason, a new date and something usable now. Asking early leaves the brand room to plan, and a firm new date is easier to agree to than an open delay.

Published 20 November 2026

When should you ask for more time on a UGC job?

Ask the same day you realise the date is at risk, not the day it falls due, because early notice keeps every option open for the brand.

The brand may have an edit, a launch or another person's work waiting on your files. A message sent days ahead lets them move that around. A message sent on the morning of the deadline leaves them only a missed date. If you aren't sure yet, say so: a heads-up that a date is at risk is still more useful than silence.

Some risks are visible before you film. If the brief changed, the product hasn't arrived, or you are still waiting on script approval, raise it then. Writing the job out as a script first, as in how to turn a brand brief into a script, shows you how much filming there really is before you commit to a date.

What should the extension message include?

Include four things: what is late, a short honest reason, the exact new date, and what you can send before then.

Keep the reason to one line. The brand needs to know whether the delay is on your side or theirs, not the whole story. Give a specific date rather than a few more days, so there is one thing to say yes to. Then offer part of the work early, so the brand isn't left with nothing.

PartWhat it doesExample wording
What is lateNames the exact deliverable, so nobody has to guess.The second video for [PRODUCT] won't be ready on [DATE].
The reasonSays in one line why, without excuses or detail.The product arrived later than planned, so filming starts tomorrow.
The new dateGives one date you are sure you can keep.I can deliver it by [DATE].
What comes firstGives the brand something to work with now.The first video is done, and I can send it today.

Pick a new date with a margin built in. A second extension is harder to ask for than a slightly later first one.

What can you write when you ask?

Use a short message that leads with the change, then gives the new date and the offer, and ends with a clear question.

  • Hi [NAME], a quick update on [PRODUCT]. I won't make [DATE] for the final videos, because the product arrived later than planned.
  • I can deliver all of them by [DATE]. The first one is filmed, so I can send it today if that helps your edit.
  • Does that new date work for you, or is there a part you need sooner?
  • If the date is fixed on your side, tell me which video matters most and I'll finish that one first.
  • Thanks for your patience, [NAME].

Read it back before you send it and cut any line that apologises twice or explains more than once. A short message reads as someone in control of the job.

What can you offer if the brand can't move the date?

Offer a smaller delivery on time and the rest later, so the brand gets what it needs most by the original date.

Ask which deliverable matters most and finish that one first. Other options are to send the main cut on time and the extra hooks after, or to deliver clean footage first and the edit later. Whatever you agree, confirm it in writing in the same thread, so the new scope and dates are clear to both sides.

If none of that works, say so plainly and let the brand decide. Handing the choice back is better than promising a date you'll miss again. For how changes and extra rounds fit into a job, see how revisions should work on a UGC job.

How do you avoid needing an extension next time?

Check the timeline against your real schedule before you agree to it, and note what you are waiting on from the brand.

  • Before you say yes, list every step that happens off camera, such as unboxing, set-up, editing and exporting, because each one adds to the job.
  • Write down what the deadline depends on, such as the product arriving or script approval, and agree a later date if those slip.
  • Confirm the brief and the deliverables before filming, using how to confirm a brief before you start filming.
  • Send a short progress note halfway, so a slip shows up early instead of on the due date.

When you deliver, check the list in what you should deliver at the end of a UGC job, so a missing file doesn't become a second delay.
