How do you write a hook for a product most people already know?

Write a hook for a familiar product by opening on a specific moment from your own life, not on the product category. Skip the line everyone could say, find one small situation or detail only you have filmed, and let the product arrive as the answer to it a few seconds later.

Published 21 November 2026

Why is a hook for an everyday product harder to write?

Naming a familiar product tells the viewer nothing new, so the hook has to carry something they have not seen before.

With a new gadget, showing it can be the surprise. With a towel, a kettle or a toothbrush, the viewer already has one, so a line like this is the best towel I own gives them no reason to keep watching. The fix is to move the hook away from the product and onto a moment around it: the mess, the habit, the small annoyance, the odd place it ended up. That moment is yours, so it is new even when the product is not.

This also changes where the product goes. For a familiar item it often works to hold the name back for a beat or two; should a UGC hook mention the product at all? walks through that choice in more depth.

Where do you find a fresh angle on a familiar product?

Look at the moments before, during and after you use the product, and pick the one that is most specific to your home.

Use the product for a few days with your phone nearby and write down anything that makes you pause. The hook shapes in the main hook guide still apply; what changes is where the material comes from. These four sources give you a starting point.

Every line in the right-hand column is something you would have to see or live to write. That is the test: if any creator could say the line without owning the product, find a more specific moment.

How do you turn a small detail into a hook line?

Write the obvious line first, cross it out, then rewrite it around the one detail you can show in the first shot.

  • Write the generic version, such as I love this towel, so you know what you are moving away from.
  • List three moments from your notes where the product, or the problem it fixes for you, was in the room.
  • Pick the moment with a picture you can film in one shot, because the first frame has to back up the first line.
  • Rewrite the line so it names the moment, not the product, and keep it to one breath.
  • Say it out loud to camera and cut any word you stumble on.

Write two or three versions from different moments and film them all, so the brand can choose. How many hooks should you film for one UGC video? covers how to shoot the alternatives without reshooting the whole video.

How do you check the hook does not oversell an ordinary product?

Check that the rest of the video shows exactly what the hook sets up, and nothing bigger, before you send it.

With an everyday product it is tempting to inflate the opening to make up for the product being plain, with lines like you have been doing this wrong your whole life. If the video then shows a normal towel on a hook, the payoff falls short and the viewer feels tricked. Read your hook, then watch the last few seconds, and ask whether the ending answers the opening in full. Hook and payoff has a quick way to audit that match.

Last, read the hook against the brief. If the brand asks for the product to be seen in the first frame, keep your moment and put the product inside it, as in this answer on a product that has to open the video, rather than dropping the moment altogether.
