How do you script a before-and-after UGC video honestly?

An honest before-and-after UGC video films the same real thing twice, in the same light from the same spot, and shows the work that happened in between. Script the middle as carefully as the reveal, say plainly how much time passed, and never film an after that you set up rather than earned.

Published 1 November 2026

What makes a before-and-after UGC video honest?

A before-and-after is honest when both shots show the same real thing under the same conditions, and the change between them is something you actually did.

The viewer judges this format by comparing two frames, so anything that differs apart from the product becomes a question. Brighter light, a closer angle or a tidier background all make the after look better for reasons that have nothing to do with what you are showing. The shape is close to the one in how to script a problem-solution UGC video, with one difference: the proof is a direct visual comparison, so the camera has to be trusted more than your words. Why does the in-between matter more than the reveal?

The in-between matters because it is the part that shows you did the work, and without it the reveal looks like two unrelated clips.

Treat the middle as two or three short beats rather than a montage. Each beat should show one step with the product in your hand, so the viewer learns how it is used while they wait for the result. Speak during these beats about what you are doing and why, not about how good it will look. If you cut time out, say so in a line such as I left it overnight, because an unexplained jump is where doubt starts.

How do you keep the light and angle the same?

Keep the phone fixed in one place and film both shots at the same time of day, so the only thing that changes is the subject.

  • Mount the phone on a stand or prop it against something heavy, and mark where it stands with a piece of tape in case you have to move it.
  • Film near the same window at the same hour, or switch every lamp off and use one light you control for both shots.
  • Write the framing into your shot list before the before shot, including the zoom, so you can match it later.
  • Leave the background exactly as it was. If you tidy the room, do it before the before shot, not between the two.
  • Watch the before clip again just before you film the after, so you can line the frame up by eye.

What does a before-and-after script look like?

A before-and-after script opens on the before, spends most of its beats on the work, and lets the matching after shot do the talking.

Every spoken line is something the creator did or saw. The comparison in the turn carries the result, so the script never has to describe it.

What should you do when the change is slow or small?

When the change is slow or small, show it at its real size and say how long it took, rather than making it look bigger than it was.

Never stage an after by cleaning, editing or swapping the subject off camera. If the brief asks for a result you have not seen, ask the brand whether a different shape would suit, such as a testimonial-style video that talks about what you noticed without a split comparison.
