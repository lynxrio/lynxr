How to write a POV video script

A POV video script is a script for a scene in which the camera stands in for someone's eyes, usually the viewer's. To write one for a brand, decide who the camera is, say so in a short on-screen label, and bring the product in at the moment that person would actually reach for it.

Published 7 October 2026

What is a POV video?

A POV video is a scene filmed so the viewer is inside it, introduced by an on-screen label. POV stands for point of view, and the label tells the viewer whose point of view the camera is borrowing before anyone speaks.

It is a format, which means its shape carries from one product to the next while the scene changes. What a UGC video format is explains that split between shape and subject. A storytime tells the viewer about something that already happened. A POV video plays a moment out as it happens, with the viewer in it. How to script a storytime video for a brand covers the first, and this guide covers the second.

Who is the camera supposed to be?

The camera is either the viewer or the person the viewer is following, and the script has to pick one before anything else gets written.

When the camera is the viewer, the creator plays someone speaking to them: a friend, a colleague, a customer, a version of themselves from another day. Every line is addressed to “you” and the creator looks into the lens. When the camera is the creator's own eyes, the viewer sees what the creator sees, usually their hands and whatever they are holding, and the lines are the creator's reactions to it.

Pick one and keep it for the whole script. A video that opens with the creator playing someone talking to “you” and then drifts into describing their own morning has swapped whose eyes the camera is, and never told the viewer.

What does the POV: label do?

The label names the situation the viewer is in before the first line is spoken. It is the quickest way to tell the viewer who they are in this scene.

That makes the label part of the hook. On a video with no spoken opening it is the hook. On a video with one, the two should not repeat each other: let the label set the situation and let the first line be a character reacting to it.

Write the label as a situation, never as a product. “POV: you're the friend whose phone just died” sets a scene. “POV: you need a portable charger” is an ad wearing a label. Keep it short enough to be read at a glance, and write it in the second person when the camera is the viewer.

What does the beat map of a POV video look like?

A POV script uses the same five parts as any short-form script, with every part kept inside the scene. Fill these in with one line each before writing any dialogue.

PartWhat happensIts job
HookThe label goes up, and the first line or action drops the viewer into the situation.Tells the viewer who they are and what is happening to them.
SetupOne line or action adds what is at stake in the scene.Gives the situation a reason to matter.
BeatsThe character reacts, asks or pushes, and the viewer's silence is part of the scene.Moves the scene forward without stepping out of it.
TurnThe product enters as something the character uses, notices or gets asked about.Changes how the scene goes.
CloseThe scene ends in character, with the product still in it.Ends the moment and leaves room for at most one ask.

Where does the product go in a POV video?

The product goes at the turn, as part of the scene rather than as a message to the camera.

In a POV video the turn is the moment the scene needs something and the product supplies it. The character reaches for it, notices it, or gets asked about it, and the scene goes differently afterwards. Nobody in the scene explains what it is. Mention it once, in the words that character would use, and move on.

If the product cannot enter the scene without someone describing it to the camera, this is the wrong scene for this brand. Choose another situation instead of bending the first one.

How does a POV script go wrong?

The failure specific to this format is a POV label on a video that is not a POV video.

A label is a promise that the viewer is inside a scene. If the creator then looks into the lens as themselves and lists what the product does, the label promised a scene and the video delivered a pitch. To test a script, cover the label and read what is left. If there is no scene without it, the label was decoration.

The second failure is changing the camera partway through. A script that starts as a scene with the viewer and ends with the creator talking about their own routine has two formats in it, and the viewer feels the join.
