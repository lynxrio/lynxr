How do you explain what's included in your UGC price?

Explain what is included in your UGC price by listing every piece of the job as its own plain line, then adding a short line for what is not included. Name the videos, the hooks, the revision rounds and the usage, so the brand can read the scope once and never has to guess.

Published 25 November 2026

Why does the wording of what is included matter so much?

Clear inclusions stop a price from meaning one thing to you and another to the brand.

A price on its own answers nothing. The brand reads it against what they think they are getting, and you read it against what you plan to make. If the two pictures differ, the gap shows up later as an extra edit, a missing file or an awkward conversation about usage. Writing the inclusions down closes that gap before it opens.

This answer is about the exact words you use. If you need help deciding the number first, start with working out what to charge for a UGC video. If you want to defend the rate as a whole, explaining your UGC rate to a brand covers that conversation. Here, the job is to make each line impossible to misread, using the same terms as the brief where you can, as in turning a brand brief into a script.

What lines should your list of inclusions have?

Give each part of the job its own line: the finished videos, any extra hooks, raw files, revisions, usage, and the delivery date.

  • Finished videos: how many, roughly how long, and in which shape, such as vertical with captions.
  • Extra hook versions: how many, and whether they share the same body.
  • The script: whether you write it, and whether it goes to the brand for approval first.
  • Revision rounds: how many, and what one round means, such as one set of changes sent together.
  • Raw files: included or not, and in what form.
  • Usage: where and for how long the brand can use the videos, agreed in writing with them.
  • Delivery: the date and how the files will arrive.

Leave out anything you would not want to be held to. A line on the list is a promise, so only list what you are happy to deliver.

How do you turn vague inclusions into clear ones?

Swap every general word for a count, a description or a condition the brand can check against the files.

Vague wordingClear wordingWhy it reads better
A UGC videoOne finished vertical video with captions and musicThe brand knows exactly what lands in the folder
Some hook optionsThree hook versions, same bodyThey can see these are openings, not new videos
Revisions includedOne round of changes, sent together in one messageIt sets the shape of feedback, not only the count
Raw footage availableRaw clips not included; available as an add-onThe default is stated instead of implied
Usage includedUsage on the brand's own organic posts for [NUMBER] months, as agreed with youScope and time are both on the page

Read each line as if you were the brand and had never spoken to you. If a line could still mean two things, add one more word until it cannot.

Should you list what is not included as well?

Add a short not-included line, because it answers the questions a brand might otherwise only ask after the job starts.

You do not need a long list. Name the two or three things a brand might assume come with the price, such as raw footage, paid ad usage or extra edits after the agreed round. For each one, say it is available and that you will quote it if they want it. Whether to charge extra for raw footage helps you decide how to treat that line.

Here is wording you can adapt: Not included: raw clips, paid ad usage, and changes after the agreed round. Happy to quote any of these if you need them, [NAME].

How do you answer when a brand asks whether something is included?

Point to the line in your list that answers it, give a straight yes or no, and offer a quote if the answer is no.

A brand asking does that include a cut-down version is a good sign: they read the scope. Answer in one or two sentences. If it is in the list, say yes and quote the line. If it is not, say it is not part of this price and give them the add-on option, so the conversation stays about scope and not about whether you are being difficult.

  • If it is in the list: Yes, that is covered by the line on [PRODUCT] hook versions.
  • If it is not: That is not in this price, but I can add it for [AMOUNT] and keep the same delivery date.
  • If you are not sure yet: Let me check what that would take and come back to you by [DATE].

Then update the written scope, so your list stays the single version both of you work from. For usage questions in particular, what usage rights mean in UGC gives you the words to describe the options.

Where should the list of inclusions live?

Put it in the quote you send and repeat it in the confirmation before work starts, so the same words follow the job from start to finish.

Keep one master version you copy from, and adjust only the counts and dates for each brand. That way your wording stays consistent and you do not reinvent it every time. If you use a rate card, make sure its descriptions match your inclusions word for word, so nothing on the card contradicts the quote.
