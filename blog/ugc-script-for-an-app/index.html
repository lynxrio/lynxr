How do you write a UGC script for an app?

A UGC script for an app is built around one moment on the screen where the app does something useful for you. Open on the everyday situation that sends you to your phone, show that single moment in a screen recording, and pair every spoken line with a clear instruction for what the phone or your hand is doing.

Published 14 October 2026

Why is an app harder to script than a physical product?

An app lives on a small screen, so the camera has nothing to pick up, touch or turn over unless you plan every shot.

With a jar or a bag, the object carries the video and you can improvise around it. With an app, a viewer who cannot read the screen sees a person staring at a phone. That is why an app script needs a picture written for every line, and why the two-column script suits it so well: one column for what you say, one for what the phone shows at that moment.

What should an app script show instead of a feature tour?

Show one moment of value: the single tap or screen where the app does the job you opened it for.

A tour of five features turns into a slideshow, and the viewer cannot hold any of them. Pick the moment you would show a friend if they asked what the app is for, and build the whole video around reaching it. Everything before that moment is the situation that sent you to the phone; everything after is your honest reaction and a call to action. The parts of a UGC video script map neatly onto this: the moment of value is your turn.

If the brief lists several features, ask the brand which one matters most for this video. You can offer to cover the others in separate videos, each with its own moment.

How do you write the camera lines for an app video?

Write each camera line as one of three shots: your face, the phone in your hand, or a full-screen recording.

ShotWhen to use itWhat to write in the camera line
Face to cameraThe hook and your reactionWhere you are and what you are holding
Phone in handOpening the app and the first tapWhich hand, which angle, and what the thumb presses
Screen recordingThe moment of valueThe exact screen and the tap that gets there
Back to faceThe closePhone lowered, eyes back on the lens

Record the screen part separately and cut it in, because a bright phone screen filmed with a second camera can look washed out. Before you film, set the app up with realistic but made-up details, so nothing private shows and the screen looks lived in.

What should you check before you send an app script to the brand?

Check that every line has a shot, the moment of value is on screen, and the app's real screens match what you wrote.

  • Every spoken line has a camera line next to it, so nothing leaves the viewer looking at a still face and a hidden phone.
  • The moment of value appears as a screen recording, not only described out loud.
  • Each button you name exists in the current version of the app, because screens change between versions.
  • Nothing on screen shows your real contacts, messages or account details.
  • The close asks for one thing, and it is the action the brief asks for.
