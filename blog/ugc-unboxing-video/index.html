How do you script an unboxing UGC video?

An unboxing UGC script is built around the moment you first see the product inside, not the outside of the box. Open on that moment or a promise of it, spend as little time as possible on tape and cardboard, give each layer of the packaging its own beat, and end with the product in use and one clear ask.

Published 24 October 2026

What makes an unboxing video worth watching?

An unboxing is worth watching when it gives the viewer the first look they would get if the parcel were theirs.

Nobody stays to watch tape being cut. They stay because they want to see what is inside, how it is packed, and whether it looks the way it did online. That makes the unboxing a format with one built-in payoff: the reveal. Everything in your script should either build toward that reveal or show what comes straight after it.

The shape still follows the parts of a UGC video script: a hook, a short setup, beats, a turn and a close. An unboxing just fills each part with a layer of the box instead of a story.

Should the hook show the box or the product?

The hook should show or promise the first look inside, because a closed box gives the viewer nothing yet to stay for.

A sealed parcel on a table looks like every other parcel. Better options are a flash of the product before you rewind to the box, a line about what you expect to find, or the moment the lid lifts with the reaction in frame. Any shape from the guide to writing a scroll-stopping hook can carry this, provided your hook aims at what is inside.

What beats should an unboxing script follow?

Give each layer of the packaging one beat, from the outer box to the product in your hand, and cut anything that doesn't change the picture.

  • The outside, only if it matters: a box the brief wants seen, or packaging that is part of the product. Otherwise skip it.
  • The first layer: the lid lifting, tissue folding back, or the top of the product appearing. Keep the camera close and quiet.
  • The product out of the box: in your hand, turned slowly so the viewer sees the front, the side and any detail the brief names.
  • The extras: what else came with it, shown together in one shot rather than one at a time.
  • The first use: switching it on, putting it on, pouring it, or setting it where it will live. This is the turn.

Film each beat as a separate clip, so you can trim the boring frames between them and re-shoot one layer without opening a fresh box. Check the brief before you start, in case the brand wants the packaging kept for a reshoot or sent back unopened.

What does an unboxing UGC script look like written out?

A written unboxing script lists what you say and what the camera sees for each layer, with the first use as the turn.

Every line says what you did or checked, and nothing promises what the set will do for the viewer's packing. That keeps it honest and keeps the reveal as the reason to watch.

How is an unboxing different from other UGC formats?

An unboxing gets its tension from packaging and the first look, while other formats get it from a problem, a story or a comparison.

That makes it a good fit for products that look good the moment they are seen, and a weaker fit for products whose value only shows after a week of use. If the brief needs the second kind, ask the brand whether a routine or a before-and-after style would serve it better.
