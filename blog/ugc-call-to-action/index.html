How do you write a call to action for a UGC ad?

A good call to action in a UGC ad is one short ask in the close that follows straight from the last thing you showed. Pick the single action the brief wants, say it in your own words, and tie it to the moment just before it, so the ask feels like the end of the story rather than a sales line bolted on.

Published 6 November 2026

Where does the call to action go in a UGC script?

The call to action goes in the close, right after the payoff, and it should take one sentence.

By the time you ask, the viewer has seen the problem, the product and the moment it made sense. That is the only point where an ask feels earned, because they now have a reason to act. Put it earlier and you are asking before you have shown anything. The call to action belongs inside the close, and the parts of a UGC video script shows how the close sits after every other part.

Keep it to one sentence and one breath. If the close needs a second sentence, the second one is often explaining something the video should already have shown, so move that into an earlier beat instead.

Why should a UGC ad ask for only one thing?

Ask for one thing, because a viewer who hears two asks has to choose, and a choice at the end of a video is easy to skip.

A close like follow me, save this and tap the link gives the viewer three jobs and no reason to do any of them. One ask lets you build the last beat around it. Read the brief before you write a word, because the brief should name the action the brand is paying for, and that choice decides your close. If the brief is unclear, ask the brand which single action matters most, and write the close only after you hear back. If the brand insists on two asks, put the one it cares about most last, because the last line is the one the viewer is left holding.

What should the call to action say for each goal?

The wording depends on what the brand wants the viewer to do, so match the verb to that action and give the viewer a reason drawn from your video.

GoalLine that sounds earnedWhy it works
Tap the linkIf your mornings look like my before, it's linked.It points back to the problem the viewer just recognised.
Save the videoSave this for the next time you're packing in a hurry.It names the moment the viewer will need it again.
CommentTell me which drawer in your house is the worst one.It asks a question only the viewer can answer.
Shop nowThe one I use is the small one, and it's in my link.It narrows the choice so the viewer knows what to pick.
Use a codeMy code is [NAME] if you want to try it.It is short, and the brief supplies the exact code.

Notice that each line refers to something already in the video. That link back is what keeps the ask from sounding like a voiceover read by someone else.

How do you make a call to action sound earned instead of salesy?

Write the ask as the last line of your story, in your own voice, and cut every word only a sales page would say.

  • Reuse a detail from the hook, because it closes the loop the viewer has been waiting on.
  • For link and shop asks, say what you use before you say where to find it, so the ask reads as a tip from someone who tried it.
  • Drop words like amazing, life-changing and must-have, because they make the close sound copied from an ad.
  • Say the line out loud before you film, and rewrite anything you would not say to a friend.
  • Keep the camera on the product or the result while you speak, so the viewer has something to look at besides your face.
