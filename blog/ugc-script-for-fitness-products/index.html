How do you write a fitness UGC script without promising results?

A fitness UGC script sells the routine you can film, not a body you cannot promise. Show where the product fits in a real session, say what you noticed while using it, and keep every line about your own experience, so the viewer judges the product and not a result nobody can see yet.

Published 2 November 2026

Why do fitness UGC scripts drift into promises?

Results depend on the person, so a line about how someone's body will change is a promise you cannot film.

The pull toward promises starts with the opening line. A fitness hook wants a big reason to keep watching, and the quickest big reason is a body that looks different. Resist it before you write a word. Read the brief for the lines you must use and the claims you must avoid, and treat that list as fixed. Marking up the brief is covered step by step in how to turn a brand brief into a script, and doing it first means nothing slips through once you start drafting.

Then write every line in the first person and about the past or the present: what you did, what you noticed, what you changed in your week. That keeps the script about your experience, which you can stand behind, instead of the viewer's future, which you can't.

What can a fitness script show instead of results?

It can show the routine, the effort, the setup and how the product feels in use, because all of those happen in front of the camera.

The formats that suit this are process formats: a routine walkthrough, one session from start to finish, a setup video, or a POV video of the moment you decide to train anyway. Each one is built from things you did, so none of them needs a claim to hold the viewer. How to write a POV video script covers that last one in detail.

What should you say instead of a result?

Say what you noticed, what you did and what you would tell a friend, because those are true for you and need no promise.

  • Swap outcome words for sensation words. Instead of a line about getting toned, say where you felt the work, such as in your shoulders by the last set.
  • Describe the product, not the person. Talk about the grip, the weight, the noise it makes, how it packs away and how quickly you can set it up.
  • Describe your routine honestly. Say how often you use it in plain words, such as twice a week before work, and say nothing about where that routine will take anyone else.
  • Keep comparisons to your own past. You can say you used to skip sessions because the gym felt far away, as long as it is true for you.
  • Hand the claim back to the brand. If it wants a benefit mentioned, ask for the exact approved wording in writing and say only that.

If a line still sounds like a promise, test it against the camera: could you film proof of it in this video? If not, cut it or turn it into something you noticed. The same test sits behind hook and payoff, because a hook can only promise what the rest of the video goes on to show. It also runs through what goes in a UGC script for a skincare brand, where results are just as personal and just as slow to film.

What does an honest fitness UGC script look like?

An honest fitness script follows one real session, lets the product appear where it actually fits, and closes by asking for one thing.

Notice what it never says. Nothing about how the creator's legs look, nothing about what the viewer will gain, and no line the camera can't back up. Lay your draft out as a two-column script like this one, so the brand can check every spoken line against what is on screen before you film.

What if the brand asks you to promise results?

Ask the brand for its approved claim wording in writing and use only that, because a claim is the brand's to make and stand behind, not yours.

If the request arrives as a note on a draft, reply with the line you can say and your reason for it. A short message keeps it calm: “I can only speak for my own sessions, so I'd rather not say how this will change anyone's body. I can describe what I noticed while using [PRODUCT], or use the wording your team has approved if you send it over.” That gives the brand two ways forward instead of a refusal.
