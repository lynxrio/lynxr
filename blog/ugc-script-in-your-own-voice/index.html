How do you write a UGC script in your own voice?

Write a UGC script in your own voice by saying the brand's copy out loud, then rewriting each line the way you would explain it to a friend. Keep any required claim exactly as the brief words it, and change only the words around it, so the video sounds like you and still says what the brand approved.

Published 14 November 2026

Where do you start when the brief is full of brand copy?

Start by sorting the copy into lines you must keep word for word and lines you are free to rephrase, because only the second group is yours to change.

Go through the brief with two highlighters. Mark the required claims, product names and any wording the brand says to use exactly. Mark everything else as a message to get across rather than a line to read. Turning a brand brief into a script covers where to put the fixed lines once you know which they are.

If you cannot tell whether a line is fixed, ask the brand before you write: is this wording required, or is it the idea you want across? That one question saves a rewrite after filming.

How do you find out what your own voice sounds like?

Record yourself explaining the product to a friend without notes, then write down the words you actually used, because that recording is your voice on paper.

Talk for a minute about why you picked the product up and what you noticed. Then play it back and copy out your phrases: how you start a sentence, the words you repeat, where you pause, what you call things. If it comes out shorter and plainer than the brand copy, that is a good sign. Keep that list next to you while you write, and draw on it before you reach for a word from the brief.

  • Note how you open a thought, such as so, okay or right, because it becomes the start of your lines.
  • Note the everyday name you give the product, like the basket or this thing, and use it in the gaps between required lines.
  • Note any phrase you say that you would never write, since those are the ones that make a script sound spoken.

How do you rewrite a brand line in your own words?

Ask what the line is trying to tell the viewer, then say that one point the way you said it in your recording.

Copy written to be read can pack several features into one sentence, while a spoken line is easier to follow when it carries one idea. Split long lines, swap formal words for the ones you use, and turn a feature into something you did with it. If the script still sounds stiff when you read it aloud, the advice in how to write a script that does not sound scripted helps with delivery once the wording is yours.

How do you keep required claims intact without sounding stiff?

Leave the required claim exactly as written and build your own words around it, because the surrounding lines are what make it sound natural.

Put a line of your own just before the claim to set it up, and one just after to show it. The claim itself stays word for word. For example, if the brief requires folds flat, you can say: my old basket lived in the hallway. This one folds flat. Watch, it goes behind the door. Only the middle line is the brand's, and it now sounds like part of your thought.

Never soften, stretch or add to a required claim to make it fit your voice. If a fixed line feels impossible to say naturally, tell the brand which line and why, and ask whether a close version is allowed. It reads like a short story about your day with the product, with the brand's fixed wording dropped in where it belongs.
