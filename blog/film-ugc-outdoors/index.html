How do you film UGC outdoors without it looking messy?

To film UGC outdoors without it looking messy, pick a calm spot with a plain background, film at a quiet time, and keep the light the same in every shot. Plan your shots before you go, so you spend the time filming rather than deciding, and check each take for strangers, wind noise and clutter before you pack up.

Published 12 November 2026

How do you choose a spot that looks clean on camera?

Choose a spot with one plain background, like a wall or a hedge, because a busy background pulls the eye away from you and the product.

  • Stand where you plan to film and look at the screen, not the view. A place that feels pretty in person can look cluttered in a tall frame.
  • Look for a background that is a few steps behind you. Space between you and it keeps the focus on you and makes signs or bins less sharp.
  • Check what moves behind you, such as traffic, a flag or a busy door. Movement in the background keeps catching the eye.
  • Pick somewhere the brief actually fits. A product used in the kitchen looks odd being shown off in a park.
  • Scout the spot once at the time of day you plan to film, so the light and the crowd match what you will get.

What should you plan before you leave the house?

Plan every shot on a written shot list before you leave, because outdoor time runs out fast once the light shifts or people turn up.

Write the script out with what you say on one side and what the camera sees on the other, the way the two-column script lays it out. That column of camera notes becomes your shot list. Mark which lines need you talking to camera and which are close-ups of the product, then group them by where you stand, so you film everything at one spot before you move. Pack a spare cloth for wiping the product, a bag to hide your things in, and a way to prop the phone so you are not relying on a stranger to hold it.

How do you deal with wind and outdoor noise?

Deal with wind by standing with your back to it and filming close to the microphone, because wind hitting the mic head-on is the sound that ruins outdoor takes.

Before you film the whole thing, record one line and play it back with headphones. If you hear rumbling, move behind a wall or a building, or wait for a calmer moment. If the noise is traffic or chatter, film the talking lines somewhere quieter and keep the busy spot for close-ups with no speech. The answer on getting clear audio goes deeper on testing sound before a take. Wind also moves your hair and anything light in the shot, so tie hair back and hold labels and packaging firmly.

How do you keep strangers out of the frame?

Keep strangers out of frame by filming at quiet times, framing tighter, and pointing the camera away from paths and doors where people walk through.

How do you make outdoor shots match each other?

Make outdoor shots match by filming them close together in time, from the same side of the light, with the same framing. Shots taken apart look like different days stitched together.

  • Face the light the same way in every talking shot, so shadows fall on the same side of your face.
  • If a cloud covers the sun mid-take, wait for it to pass and refilm, rather than mixing bright and dull shots.
  • Lock the exposure on your phone once you have the look you want, so it does not shift every time you move.
  • Keep your clothes, hair and the product in the same state across every shot, and take a quick photo to check against.
  • Film any extra cutaways at the same spot right after the main takes, using the ideas in the answer on what B-roll to film.

What should you check before you pack up?

Before you pack up, watch every planned shot back once, with sound, against your shot list. Going back to the same spot on another day rarely gives you the same light.

Tick off each line of the list only when you have a take with clean sound, no strangers, the product in focus and the label readable. Film one spare take of the hardest line, because outdoor takes can look fine on a small screen and show a problem later on a bigger one. Then sort the clips while you still remember which take was best, which the answer on organising footage walks through.
