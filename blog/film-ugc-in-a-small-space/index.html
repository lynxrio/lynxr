How do you film UGC in a small space?

Film UGC in a small space by choosing one corner, pointing the camera along the longest line in the room, and framing tighter than feels natural. A small room only looks small when the edges show, so plan close shots, keep the background to one tidy surface, and move the product rather than yourself.

Published 17 November 2026

Where in a small room should you film?

Film from the spot that lets the camera look along the longest line in the room, usually corner to corner, so there is depth behind you.

Depth behind the subject is what makes a frame feel open. If the camera faces a wall a step away, the shot looks flat and cramped however tidy it is. Stand in each corner with your phone and look at the screen, not the room. Pick the angle that shows the most distance and the fewest objects, and mark where the phone sat with a small piece of tape so you can return to it on the next brief.

Light matters as much as space here. How do you light a UGC video at home covers facing a window, which often decides the corner for you.

How should you frame shots when there is no room to step back?

Frame tighter, so the edges of the room never enter the shot, and let the product and your hands fill most of the screen.

In a small space you can't back away to fit everything in, and a wide lens on a phone bends the walls when it's close. Close framing avoids both. Plan each shot so it shows only what that beat needs, which also keeps the viewer looking at the product instead of the room. Writing the script as a two-column script, with what you say beside what the camera sees, lets you plan each frame before you set up.

If a shot needs your whole body, try it in a doorway or hallway, where the length of the space gives you the distance a room can't.

How do you keep a small background from looking cluttered?

Choose one surface behind you, clear it, and add a single object that fits the product, so the frame reads as deliberate rather than crowded.

A small room puts everything close to the lens, so a pile of laundry or a busy shelf takes up a lot of the frame. Clearing the whole room isn't necessary. Clear only the part the camera sees, which you can check on screen in a minute or two. Keep a short routine for it:

  • Look at the frame on your phone, not the room, and remove anything that pulls your eye.
  • Leave one or two things that make sense next to the product, such as a mug beside a kitchen item.
  • Turn off overhead lights that make shadows on the wall behind you, if a window gives enough light.
  • Take a photo of the cleared frame, so a later reshoot can match it.

How do you film movement and demos in a tight space?

Move the product to the camera instead of moving the camera around the room, and break any action into short separate shots.

Walking shots and wide pans need space you don't have. Short clips filmed from one or two fixed positions look cleaner and are easier to cut together. Write the demo as a list of shots before you start, so you aren't rearranging furniture between takes. A shot list for a small room might look like this:

  • Close shot of the product on a clear surface, then your hand picking it up.
  • The product moving toward the lens to show one feature.
  • Top-down shot of it in use on the table or floor.
  • A talking shot, chest up, in the corner you chose earlier.

Each of these can be filmed without moving the phone more than a step. For extra cutaways to fill the edit, what B-roll should you film for a UGC video is worth reading alongside this.

What can you use instead of a tripod or stand in a small room?

Anything stable at the right height will do, as long as the phone stays still and the angle is the same between takes.

A stack of books on a shelf, a mug with the phone propped against it, or the back of a chair can all hold a phone steady. What matters is that you can put it back in exactly the same place. Mark the spot, and check the frame before every take. For top-down shots, a shelf above a table or a phone laid on a board between two chairs can work, if it can't slip.

Keep sound in mind too. A small room with hard walls can make your voice echo, so soft things nearby such as curtains, cushions or a bed can help. How do you get clear audio in a UGC video goes into this in more detail.

How do you check the space works before the real shoot?

Film a quick test of each shot, watch it back on your phone, and fix what you see before filming the full script.

A test shows what the room looks like to the viewer, which is often different from how it feels when you stand in it. Look for walls bending at the edges, a corner of the ceiling showing, clutter creeping into the frame and shadows behind you. Change one thing at a time, film again and compare. Once the setup works, note it down with a photo, so the next job starts from a setup you already know.
