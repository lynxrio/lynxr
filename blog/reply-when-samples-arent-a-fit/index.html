How do you reply when a brand says your samples aren't a fit?

Reply to a not-a-fit message with a short thank-you, one specific question about what the brand was looking for, and an offer to share relevant work later. Keep it brief and free of argument, then use whatever the brand tells you to choose or film samples that match the next pitch more closely.

Published 28 November 2026

Should you reply at all when a brand says no?

Reply, because a short, polite answer costs you a minute and leaves the person with a good last impression of you.

A not-a-fit message is about the samples you sent for this job, not a verdict on whether you can do the work. The person who wrote it may handle other products, move to another team, or brief a different campaign next season. Silence tells them nothing. A calm reply shows how you handle feedback, which is part of what a brand weighs when it works with a creator. What you leave out matters as much: no defending the samples, no listing your other skills, no asking them to reconsider.

What do you actually write back?

Write three or four sentences: thank them, ask one easy question, and say you would be glad to share closer work in future.

  • Hi [NAME], thanks for taking the time to look, and for letting me know.
  • So my next samples are more useful to you, was it the style, the product category or the way I'm on camera that didn't match what you need for [PRODUCT]?
  • If I make something closer to that, I'd be happy to send it over, but no pressure either way.
  • Thanks again, and good luck with the campaign.

Giving them a short menu in the question makes it easy to answer in a line. An open question like any feedback asks them to write something, and a busy person can skip it. If they do reply, read it next to your samples, the same way you would treat notes after a finished job in asking a brand for feedback. How do you work out why the samples missed?

Compare your samples with the brand's own recent videos and its request, and look for the gap in category, format, look or delivery.

The brand may never explain, so you can do the check yourself. Scroll the videos it posts on TikTok or Instagram and put one of your samples beside them. The table below covers the gaps that are easiest to spot and the change each one points to.

What you noticeWhat it might meanWhat to change next time
Your samples show a different product categoryThey couldn't picture you with their productFilm one sample with a similar kind of product
Their videos use a different formatYour samples show a different kind of videoMake a sample in the format they post
Their videos feel calmer or louder than yoursThe tone didn't match their audienceShow a second delivery style in one sample
Their videos open on the product, yours open on youThe opening didn't suit how they sellTry a sample with a different kind of hook
You sent many samples of mixed qualityThe weaker ones set the impressionSend fewer, chosen for this brand

Treat the middle column as a guess, not a fact. You are looking for something you can test, and the format and hook are the easiest parts to change on purpose.

What if the brand answers your question?

Thank them in one line, then act on the answer before you contact them again, so your next message has something new in it.

If they say the category was wrong, film a sample with a product closer to theirs. If they say the style was off, read up on what a format is and make one video in the shape they post. Before you send anything, run it through the checks in reviewing your own samples so the new piece is your strongest, not your newest. Then send it with a short note that names what you changed: you mentioned you were after calmer demos, so I filmed this one for a similar product.

When is it fine to pitch the same brand again?

Pitch again once you have new work that answers the reason they gave, or a clear change in what you can show.

Sending the same samples again asks them to say no twice. A fresh sample, a new format or a product category they work in gives them a real reason to look. Keep the second pitch short and point back to the first exchange so they remember the context, then follow the timing you would use for any follow-up on a pitch. If they still pass, take it as information about this brand and put the effort into brands whose videos already look like yours.
