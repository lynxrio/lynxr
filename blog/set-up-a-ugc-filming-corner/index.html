How do you set up a simple filming corner at home for UGC?

Set up a home filming corner by choosing one spot that is quiet and free when you work, then making it quick to rebuild. Keep everything for it in one box, decide where each piece sits, and plan two or three backgrounds you can swap, so every job starts from the same place instead of from scratch.

Published 21 November 2026

What makes a spot at home worth turning into a filming corner?

A good corner is one you can use at the hours you actually film, with steady light, little noise and a background you can change.

The best-looking room in the house is no use if someone needs it every evening. Start from your schedule, not your decor. If you film after work, pick a spot that is free then, and check it with the light you will have at that hour. If you share a home, choose somewhere away from doors, the kitchen and the washing machine, because a clean take is easier when nobody walks through it.

  • Light: a window to the side or in front of you, never behind, so your face is lit and not a silhouette.
  • Surface: a table, shelf or worktop at chest height, because so much UGC is a product in your hands or on a surface.
  • Background: a plain wall or one tidy shelf you can restyle, so a brand sees the product and not your laundry.
  • Power: a socket within reach, so a phone or light never dies halfway through a take.
  • Room to stand: enough space to move between a seated shot and a standing one without moving the phone.

You do not need to buy anything to start. What equipment do you need to start making UGC? covers the kit itself; this answer is about where that kit lives and how it comes together each time.

How do you test a spot before you commit to it?

Film a short practice clip there at the time you would really work, then watch it back on your phone with the sound on.

Write a few lines as a two-column script, so you know what to say and what to show, then say them to camera, hold up something from your kitchen, and put it down on the surface. Then check three things: whether your face is evenly lit, whether you can hear an echo or a hum, and whether anything in the frame pulls the eye away from the object in your hand. Do it twice, on a bright day and a dull one, because a corner that only works in sunshine will let you down on a deadline. How do you arrange the corner so it rebuilds in minutes?

Give every piece one fixed home and one fixed position, so setting up is putting things back where they go, not deciding again.

How do you change the background between brands?

Keep two or three backgrounds you can swap in, so one corner can look calm, warm or bright depending on what the brief asks for.

A large board painted a soft neutral, a plain throw over a chair back and a shelf you can restyle cover a lot of ground. Read the brief for clues before you choose: a mood board, a list of colours to avoid, or a line like keep it bright and clean. If it says nothing, ask the brand which look it wants, because a quick question costs less than a reshoot. Keep the props for each look together in a bag, so a skincare shelf and a kitchen counter are each one grab away.

What goes in the box that keeps the corner ready?

One box holds everything that is not furniture, so a shoot starts by opening it and ends by packing it.

  • The phone stand, its clip and a charging cable long enough to reach the socket.
  • A roll of low-tack tape for marks that peel off without damage.
  • A soft cloth for fingerprints on the lens and on shiny packaging.
  • Spare props that suit several brands: a plain mug, a neutral tray, a folded towel.
  • A printed checklist of the setup order, so a tired evening shoot still goes in the right sequence.

Packing away matters as much as setting up. A corner that takes a long time to clear is a corner you will avoid using, so put it all back in the box even when you plan to film again tomorrow.

What should you do when your filming corner stops working?

Change one thing at a time and film the test clip again, because fixing light, sound and background all at once hides which change helped.

Corners drift. The light shifts with the seasons, a new neighbour starts drilling, or a piece of furniture moves in. When a clip looks or sounds wrong, compare it with your setup photo first. If daylight is the problem, move your filming time or lean more on the lamp. If noise is the problem, add a rug or a blanket off camera. And if a brief needs a bathroom, a kitchen or the outdoors, film that job there and come back to your corner for the next one. The corner is your default, not a rule, and having one ready is what lets you say yes to a UGC job quickly.
