Do you need followers to be a UGC creator?

You do not need followers to be a UGC creator, because the brand is paying for a video it can use, not for your audience. An audience only enters the deal when the brand also wants the video posted on your account or run as an ad under your name. Build samples and a clear pitch instead.

Published 14 October 2026

What is a brand paying for if not your followers?

A brand hiring for UGC is paying for a finished video, and the right to use it, made in the style of an ordinary post.

That video goes out on the brand account or into its ads, so the people who see it are the brand's audience, not yours. What you can show a brand is that you make something that feels natural, follows the brief and holds attention. Those are skills, and you can show them on a profile with no following at all. If the term itself is new, what is a UGC creator sets out how this work differs from growing your own page.

Knowing a few repeatable shapes helps here more than a follower count would. Once you understand what a video format is, you can show a brand three different shapes for the same product and let it pick.

When does your own audience matter in a UGC deal?

Your audience matters only when the brand asks for more than the video: a post on your account, or ads that run under your name.

If an offer mixes these together, ask which one the brand wants. A small account can still say yes to a file-only deal and turn down a posting deal it is not ready for. The UGC script template is useful whichever one you take on, because the video has to work before anything else does.

What should you build instead of a following?

Build a small set of sample videos that look like real UGC, plus a short pitch that tells a brand what you can make for it.

  • Three samples in three different formats, because a brand can judge your range from them faster than from a long reel.
  • Each sample built on a product you own and have used, so what you say on camera is true.
  • A clear hook in the first line of every sample, since it is the first thing anyone watching sees.
  • One sample that follows a written brief you set yourself, so you can show you can work to instructions.
  • A single page or folder that holds the samples, so a brand opens one link and sees everything.

How do you explain a small account when you pitch a brand?

Lead with the videos you can make, and mention your account size only if the brand asks for posting.

Apologising for a small following draws attention to your account instead of your samples, so point the brand at the samples instead. Here is a line you can adapt: Hi [NAME], I make UGC videos for brands to run on their own accounts and in ads. Here are three samples: [LINK]. I would like to make one for [PRODUCT] in the same style, delivered as a file by [DATE].

For the rest of the message, writing a UGC pitch email covers what to include and what to leave out. If you are still choosing who to send it to, finding brands that hire UGC creators is the place to start.

Should you still post on your own account as a UGC creator?

Post if it helps you practise and gives brands a place to see your style, but do not wait for followers before you start pitching.

Your profile on TikTok or Instagram can work as a second portfolio. Keep it tidy, put your best samples near the top, and write a one-line bio that says you make UGC for brands. Posting your own videos also gets you comfortable on camera, which shows in every sample you send.

What you post is your choice. You can keep your personal page separate from your creator work and send samples as files only, if you would rather keep the two apart. Either way, the video is what you are putting in front of the brand.
