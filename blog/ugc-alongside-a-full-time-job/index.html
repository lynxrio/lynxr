Can you do UGC alongside a full-time job?

You can do UGC alongside a full-time job if you plan around the hours you actually have. Batch your filming into one or two fixed sessions a week, agree delivery dates with each brand before you accept, and turn down jobs whose deadlines land on your busiest days. The limit is your calendar, not your talent.

Published 8 November 2026

How much time does one UGC job really take?

One job takes more time than the filming alone, so count every step before you decide whether it fits around work.

Reading the brief, writing the script, setting up, filming, editing, sending files and handling a round of changes are all separate pieces of work. Filming is often the shortest of them. Before you take your first job, time yourself on a practice video from start to finish and write the total down, because that number is what you plan with. How do you batch UGC filming when you only have evenings and weekends?

Write every script during the week and film them all in one fixed session, so the setup only happens once.

Setting up light, sound and a clean corner is the part that eats a short evening. If you do it once on a weekend morning and film three scripts back to back, the setup cost is shared. Write each script as a shot list before the session, because then you film by ticking lines off instead of deciding on the spot. Working from a UGC script template that holds up keeps every script in the same shape, which makes them faster to film in a row.

  • Pick one filming slot a week and protect it like a meeting, because a moving slot is the first thing a busy week cancels.
  • Lay out every product and outfit the night before, so the session starts with the camera, not with a search.
  • Film the scenes that need daylight first, then the close-ups and voice lines that work under a lamp.
  • Film one extra take of each hook, because you will not get a second session to redo it before the deadline.
  • Back up the footage before you put anything away, so a full phone never costs you a session.

What should you agree with a brand when you work another job?

Agree the delivery date, the number of revision rounds and your reply times in writing before you accept the job.

You do not have to tell a brand about your day job. You do have to give dates you can keep. Work backwards from your next filming slot: the delivery date is that slot plus your editing evenings plus a spare day. Saying your reply hours up front also helps, because a brand that knows you answer in the evening is less likely to read a quiet afternoon as you going missing.

A line you can copy into your reply: Thanks [NAME], I'd love to make this. I can deliver the edited video by [DATE], with one round of changes after that. I reply to messages in the evenings, so anything you send in the day will get an answer the same night. Does that timing work for your launch?

When should you say no to a UGC job?

Say no when the deadline lands before your next filming slot, or when the job needs you somewhere during working hours.

A rushed video is worse for you than a missed job, because the brand remembers the work, not the reason it was rushed. Before you accept, check the job against your calendar rather than your enthusiasm. If it almost fits, offer a later date instead of a flat no, since a brand with a flexible launch may take it.

  • The brief asks for delivery before your next free filming session.
  • The shoot needs a location, a store or daylight that you can only reach on a workday.
  • The brand expects replies within the hour during the day.
  • You already have a job in the same week, and two would push your editing into your sleep.
  • The scope keeps growing in the messages before anything is agreed.

How do you keep UGC from spilling into your day job?

Keep the two apart with fixed hours, a separate inbox and a firm rule about what you do on work time.

Use a separate email address for brands and turn its notifications off during work hours, so a message about a revision does not pull you out of a meeting. Check your employment agreement, and ask your employer if you are unsure, about whether side work or filming at the office is allowed, and keep work devices and work premises out of your videos. Keep a simple list of every open job with its next date, because with less time the danger is forgetting a step, not running out of ideas.

Start with one job at a time until you know your real pace. Once you have finished a few, you will know how many fit in a month without a late night, and you can take on more from there. The rest of the basics are in getting started as a UGC creator.
