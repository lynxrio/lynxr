How to turn a video into a script for a brand

Turning a video into a script for a brand means finding the structure a video already ran on and running a different subject through the same structure: pick a video that already worked, write down what it does beat by beat, then swap in the new subject while keeping the shape. The result is a new script built on a pattern that already held an audience's attention, not a blank page.

What you need before you start

You need three things: a video you can watch as many times as you like — a public link is enough, no software to install — a plain description of the brand you are writing for and what they want the video to do, and a place to write down what you see, beat by beat, before you write a single line of the new script.

Step 1: pick a video that already worked

Start from a video that is already doing something well, not from a blank page — a hook that stopped you scrolling, a video that got shared, a structure you keep noticing in your own niche. It does not need to be a video about anything close to your brand; the topic is going to change. What you are picking out is the shape, not the subject.

Step 2: write down what it does, beat by beat

Watch it once for the subject, then watch it again to notice pacing and cuts. Write down the hook — what it says or shows in the first moment — and then each beat as a single line, in order: what happens, and roughly how it moves into the next thing. Note where the video turns into something else, and how it closes. Do not write any of the new words yet; just get the sequence down.

Step 3: swap the subject, keep the shape

Replace the original subject with your brand's, beat for beat, in the same order and with the same number of beats. If the original video's third beat is a demonstration, your third beat is a demonstration of your brand's product — not a testimonial, not a list, unless the original used one there. Resist the urge to add beats the original did not have: a format that worked did so as a whole shape, not as a base with extra ideas bolted on.

Step 4: rewrite the hook for the new brand

The hook is the part most worth rewriting rather than translating literally, because it has to work for a viewer who has never seen the original: it should do the same job the first hook did — stop the scroll, set up the same kind of question or claim — using your brand's own subject, not a copy of the original's exact words.

Step 5: check it still fits the platform

Read the script back against where it is going: the length the platform rewards, whether it needs on-screen text or can rely on voice alone, and whether the close matches what that platform's audience expects — a call to comment, a call to follow, a call to buy. A format borrowed from one platform does not always survive the move to another unchanged.

How long this takes by hand

By hand, this is a manual process — there is no shortcut to watching a video twice, writing its beats down, and rewriting it beat by beat for a new brand. It takes real, focused attention, and it takes longer the first few times, before writing down beats becomes a quick habit.
