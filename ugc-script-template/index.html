A UGC script template that holds up

Every UGC script that feels natural runs on the same four parts. The template is not the hard part — the hard part is what goes in each line, and what must not.

The template

Hook — one spoken line that opens a gap (see how to write a hook). Beats — two to five pairs of SAY and DO: what your mouth says, what your hands and the camera do. Turn — the moment the video becomes about the product, as late as you can afford. Close — one line that tells the viewer what to do next, plus the caption.

Writing SAY and DO as separate lines is the whole trick. A script that only writes dialogue leaves the visual channel to chance, and the visual channel is the one people actually watch.

A real example

Hook: “I went from failing my medical assessment to a 96% in two weeks.” Beat one — SAY: how it started, the failing grade. DO: face close to camera, talk fast and direct. Beat two — SAY: the one change. DO: hold the product to the lens, tap it twice. Turn: the product named exactly once. Close: “the link does the rest.”

Notice what is absent: no feature list, no “as someone who…”, no second product mention. UGC dies when it starts reading like the brand wrote it.

The three mistakes that make UGC read like an ad

Claiming a life you don't have. A script should only assert things that are true of the person reading it — invented credentials get found out in the comments.

Turning too early. If the product appears before the midpoint, the video is an ad with a cold open. The audience's patience is bought by the beats before the turn.

Closing twice. One call to action. A second one retroactively cheapens the first.
